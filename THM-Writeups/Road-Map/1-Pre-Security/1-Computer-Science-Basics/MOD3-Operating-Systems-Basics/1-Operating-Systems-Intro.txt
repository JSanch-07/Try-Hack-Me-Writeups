An operating system (OS) acts as a bridge between the user, installed software, and hardware

Learning Objectives:
	. Understand what an operating system is and the role it plays
	. Explain the core duties of an operating system
	. Identify common OS types and their typical use cases
	. Practice interacting with an OS to gather system information
	
The Invisible Manager
	. What is an operating system:
		. An OS is the core software that coordinates everything happening on a computer system
		. It acts as the "invisible manager" which keeps the entire machine running as one
			. User -> Applications -> Operating System -> Hardware
		. The "Busy Airport" Analogy:
			. Your Hardware - The runways, airplanes, fuel systems, radar
			. Your Applications - The various airlines and their passengers
			. Your Operating System - The entire air traffic control system, directing all of this activity
		. We need an OS because it provides coordination
			. This coordination is what makes modern computing possible
			. Without an OS, each application would need direct control over:
				. CPU, Memory, Files, Devices, Security, etc.
	. System Privilege Layers:
		. In modern computing, different parts of a system operate at various permission levels
			. Some components communicate directly with the hardware while others run in safer, restricted environments
			. This prevents both conflicts and security issues
		. Privilege Separation Spaces:
			. Kernel Space:
				. The privileged, locked-down core of the OS
				. This is where the kernel lives (the part of the OS which communicates directly with the hardware)
				. It has unrestricted access to the CPU, Memory, Storage, and all other components
			. User Space:
				. Where all standard applications run
				. Applications running in this space are deliberately prevented from accessing hardware directly
				. Whenever they need to perform an action outside of their permission level, they make a system call and request that the kernel act on their behalf
	. Operating System Duties:
		. Process Management:
			. Creates, schedules, prioritizes and terminates running programs
			. The OS decides how much CPU time each process gets
			. Makes multitasking feel seamless
		. Memory Management:
			. Allocates RAM to processes, protects the app's memory from other processes
			. Reclaims memory when apps are closed
			. When RAM runs low, the OS uses virtual memory to keep your machine stable
		. File System Management:
			. Organizes files into directories
			. Handles naming, paths, permissions, and metadata (name, size, type, timestamps)
		. User Management:
			. Handles multiple user accounts, authentication, and permissions to determine who can access what
		. Device Management:
			. Loads drivers and provides a universal interface (hardware abstraction layer) so apps can simply say "print {text}" 
	. Operating System Security:
		. The OS acts as a security foundation before any antivirus, firewall, etc.
		. The OS handles:
			. Authentication - Verifies who you are through methods like credentials and biometrics
			. Permissions - Controls exactly what each user and app is allowed to read, write, or execute
			. Isolation - Keeps every process in its own protected box (kernel/user space separation)
			. System Protection - Safeguards critical system files and settings from unauthorized changes

OS Interaction and Landscape
	. OS Interfaces
		. An OS interface allows the user to interact with the Operating System by means of GUI or CLI
		. Graphical User Interface (GUI):
			. What most people are used to interacting with
			. It provides a graphical representation of all the information you want to access on your computer
			. Think of windows, icons, and menus for system settings
				. GUIs allow you to tap an icon of the place you want to visit, eliminating the need for typing
		. Command-line Interface (CLI):
			. Where you enter specific text based commands to receive/manipulate information
			. Rather than clicking icons, you tell the computer exactly what you want using words and syntax that the system understands
			. This gives the user more precision and control over their inputs
				. The trade-off - Users must be well-versed with CLI syntax for their system	
	. The Operating System Landscape
		. Operating System Types and Use Cases:
			. Desktop - Personal computers, daily work, gaming, content creation:
				. Rich GUI, runs many apps at once, user focused
			. Server - Web hosting, databases, cloud services, back-end:
				. Headless (no GUI), maximum uptime, multi-user, remote access
			. Mobile - Smartphones and tablets:
				. Touch-based UI, power-efficient, always connected, app sandboxing
			. Embedded - Appliances, routers, cars, IoT devices, smart TVs:
				. Tiny footprint, runs on limited hardware
			. Virtual/Cloud - Lab machines, containers, cloud instances
				. Lightweight, scalable, rapid deployment
	. Real World Operating Systems
		. Desktop:
			. Windows: The most widely used operating system on personal computers 
				. Windows 10 (end-of-life), Windows 11
			. Apple's desktop OS, known for its polished GUI and integration with other Apple devices
				. Sonoma (14), Sequoia (15), Tahoe (26)
			. Linux: Not a single OS but a family of open-source operating systems called distributions
				. Ubuntu, Debian, Fedora
		. Server:
			. Windows: Used in large networks, data centers, and corporate environments
				. Server 2016, 2019, 2022, 2025
			. Linux: The vast majority of web servers, trusted for its reliability and open-source nature
				. Ubuntu Server, Debian, CentOS, Red Hat
			. Unix: Large enterprises, finance, telecom, government
				. IBM AIX, Oracle Solaris
		. Mobile:
			. Android: The most widely used mobile OS, which runs on phones, tablets, and smart devices
				. Android 14 - 16, Manufacturer versions
			. iOS: Apple's mobile OS running on iPhones, iPads, and other devices
				. iOS 17, 18, 26
		. Embedded/IoT Devices:
			. Embedded Linux: Specialized OS built into devices with dedicated functions
				. OpenWrt, Ubuntu Core, Yocto Project
			. Real-Time OS: Designed for apps where tasks need guaranteed response times (aircraft controls)
				. FreeRTOS, VxWorks, QNX
		. Virtual and Cloud:
			. Cloud/VM: Massive data centers that host websites, apps, and streaming services
				. Ubuntu LTS, Amazon Linux, Rocky Linux
			. Container-optimized: Lightweight alternatives to VMs that package just the app and its dependencies
				. Alpine Linux, Bottlerocket AWS, Flatcar Linux
	. Why So Many Operating Systems
		. Different devices/environments require different capabilities from an OS
			. A laptop must be user-friendly and support multitasking
			. A server requires stability, security, and must be able to run nonstop
