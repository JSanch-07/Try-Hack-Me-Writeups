Intro to OS Security:
	. Whenever you use a computer, you interact with the OS (directly or indirectly)
	. Hardware:
		. What is Hardware?
			. Hardware includes all of the physical computer parts and peripherals
				. Examples: keyboard/mouse, monitor, RAM
		. The Desktop Board:
			. Where all other hardware components connect (mouse, CPU, etc.)
		. Without an OS, hardware is useless if your goal is to run applications
	. Software vs Hardware:
		. Software - Programs (Firefox, MS-Office, Steam) and OS (Windows, iOS, Linux)
		. Hardware - CPU, RAM, SSD/HDD, Screen
	. The OS manages communication between the hardware and applications
		. The OS sits between the hardware and applications
	. Examples of OS depending on Use Case:
		. Laptop/PC - MS Windows 11 and macOS
		. Smartphone - Android and iOS
		. Servers - MS Windows Server 2022, IBM AIX, and Oracle Solaris
	. An OS must be secure for the following reasons:
		. Protect personal information
		. Protect corporate information
		. Security is concerned with attacks against:
			. Confidentiality - You want to ensure that secret and private files and information are only available to intended persons
			. Integrity - It is crucial that no one can tamper with the files stored on your system or while being transferred on the network
			. Availability - You want your laptop or smartphone to be available to use anytime you decide to use it

Common Examples of OS Security:
	. Commonly Exploited Weaknesses:
		. Authentication and weak passwords
		. Weak file permissions
		. Malicious programs
	. Authentication and Weak Passwords:
		. Authentication - The act of verifying the user's identity
			. Examples: PIN code, fingerprint, phone number + SMS
		. Top 5 Most Common Passwords:
			1. 123456
			2. 123456789
			3. qwerty
			4. password
			5. 111111
	. Weak File Permissions:
		. Proper security dictates the principle of least privilege
			. All files should only be accessible by those who need access
			. Example: Users from the customer service department should not be able to make changes which will affect the accounting department
	. Access to Malicious Programs:
		. Depending on the type of malicious program, it can attack confidentiality, integrity, and availability
			. Trojan - Gives the attacker access to your system
			. Ransomware - Where the attacker holds your data for ransom (by means of encryption) and demands payment
Practical Examples of OS Security:
	. In a typical attack, the attacker will attempt to gain access to a remote system by:
		. Obtaining credentials
		. Tricking the user into running malware
	. An attacker may attempt to escalate their privileges (administrator/root)
		. These user accounts have complete access and control over the system
		. Important commands:
			. whoami
			. ssh <username>@<machine_IP>
				. use "exit" to log out when finished
			. ls
			. cat <file_name>
			. history - Displays previously run commands
			. su <username> - switch user
		. Default Linux tools are located in the /root/Desktop/Tools and /opt/ directories
