Intro:
	. Prerequisite Knowledge:
		. What is a byte?
			. A group of 8 bits
			. Also known as an Octet
	. Learning Objectives:
		. Representing 8 colors
		. Representing 16 million colors
		. Binary numbers
		. Hexadecimal numbers

Representing Colors:
	. Our First 8 Colors (RGB):
		. You get these 8 colors by combining red, green, and blue 
		. Colors are represented as 3 digits of 1s and 0s
			. 100 = Red, 010 = Green, 001 = Blue, 110 = Red and Green are on (Yellow)
	. From 8 to 16,000,000:
		. By adding the ability to change the brightness of each light, you get more colors
			. Instead of 2 levels of brightness (on/off), we now have 256 levels allowing for a broader color profile
		. Too Many Bits:
			. One bit can represent 2 states (on/off)
			. We need 8 bits to represent the 256 states
			. This means we now need 3 X 8 bits (24 bits or 3 bytes)
				. Green = 10100011 11101010 00101010
			. Hexadecimal Representation:
				. Makes it easy to combine 4 bits into a single character 
					. 1 = 0001, 5 = 1010
					. A = 1010, E = 1110
					
				. For the color green:
					. Instead of "10100011 11101010 00101010" we can simply write "A3EA2A"
						. (1st byte) -> A3, (2nd byte) -> EA, (3rd byte) -> 2A
						. 			 10100011		   11101010		    00101010

Numbers - From Decimal to Hexadecimal:
	. Human beings have ten fingers, and this is the most plausible explanation for why we use the decimal (base-10) system in our everyday lives
	. Unlike humans, computers only have 2 states which they understand
		. 1 (on) and 0 (off)
		. Low and high voltage range (transistor-transistor logic)
		. North and South in magnetic polarity (e.g. HDD)
		. Light presence (e.g. fiber optics)
	. Transistor Example in More Detail:
		. A transistor either passes or blocks electric current
		. In digital systems, it comes down to a low or high state (representing 0 and 1)
			. Low voltage range = 0-0.8V
			. High voltage range = 2-3.3V
		. The main focus is to use these 2 digits to represent data
	. Binary Numbers:
		. In simple terms, the number 212 is the same as saying 100 + 10 + 2
		. In a more technical representation, 212 = 2x10^2 + 1x10^1 + 3x10^0
		. The binary (base-2) system can be represented in a similar way:
			. The binary number 1001 (9) = 1x2^3 + 0x2^2 + 0x2^1 + 1x2^0
		. The Underlying Pattern:
			. Binary (base-2):
				. Rightmost digit - Multiplied by 2^0
				. The next digit - Multiplied by 2^1
				. etc.
			. Decimal (base-10):
				. Rightmost digit - Multiplied by 10^0
				. The next digit - Multiplied by 10^1
				. etc.
		. Converting 1100, 1101, 1110, and 1111 to the decimal system:
			. 1100 = 1 × 2^3 + 1 × 2^2 + 0 × 2^1 + 0 × 2^0 = 1 × 8 + 1 × 4 + 0 × 2 + 0 × 1 = 8 + 4 + 0 + 0 = 12
			. 1101 = 1 × 2^3 + 1 × 2^2 + 0 × 2^1 + 1 × 2^0 = 1 × 8 + 1 × 4 + 0 × 2 + 1 × 1 = 8 + 4 + 0 + 1 = 13
			. 1110 = 1 × 2^3 + 1 × 2^2 + 1 × 2^1 + 0 × 2^0 = 1 × 8 + 1 × 4 + 1 × 2 + 0 × 1 = 8 + 4 + 2 + 0 = 14
			. 1111 = 1 × 2^3 + 1 × 2^2 + 1 × 2^1 + 1 × 2^0 = 1 × 8 + 1 × 4 + 1 × 2 + 1 × 1 = 8 + 4 + 2 + 1 = 15
